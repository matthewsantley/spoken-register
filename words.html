Where these words come from

Each word is the ordinary noun for “star”, “sky” or “moon” in the language shown. The pool was assembled with AI assistance and has been checked against published reference sources: 177 of the 229 are in those sources, four were corrected where sources disagreed, and 52 are still to be looked up. Where a source offers a related form — a base form against a definite form, a formal word against an everyday one — the row is marked variant form and carries the reason.

Reference sources catch a word that is misspelled or does not exist. They do not catch a word that is correct in a dictionary and wrong in a mouth — one that sounds archaic, or childish, or means something else entirely in the next valley. Only a speaker catches that, which is why the two columns are kept separate.

There is a pattern in the 52 not yet looked up: Cherokee, Cree, Ojibwe, Lakota, Mapudungun, Rapa Nui, Marshallese, Manx, Cornish, Kikuyu, Fula, Oromo, Tigrinya, Tamazight, Wolof. Almost all indigenous, minority or endangered. Reference tables cover the languages of large states and stop — so the words most likely to be wrong belong to the communities this is trying hardest to represent properly.

If you speak one of these languages and something here is off, the buttons on each row will tell me. It takes about a minute, and it is the only way the second column ever fills in.
